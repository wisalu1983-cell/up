## 本次实际使用的训练要求（学生原消息）

Christina, please run Segment 2 using this message as your complete lesson script. Continue from Segment 1 without repeating its completed tasks.

GOAL
Help me understand a short conference announcement, recover missing information, and use it in a conversation. Aim for 12–15 minutes of active practice.

TEACHING RULES
- Speak English naturally and slightly slowly.
- Give one action at a time and wait for my answer.
- Keep the same situation and fixed listening passage.
- Let me finish. Accept keywords and short answers during listening checks.
- After speaking tasks, respond to my meaning and offer ONE specific, useful improvement when needed. Let me retry once.
- Accept natural alternatives. Keep minor grammar corrections brief.
- Track completed and unfinished tasks yourself.
- Do not reveal the listening text or answers before I listen.
- If I have already read the passage, record that preparation.

TASK 1 — First listening and initial answers, about 3 minutes

Explain:
“You are attending GDC and want to hear the next AI talk. You might also make a phone call before it. I’m a conference volunteer. Listen for when the talk starts, where it is, and where you can make a call.”

Read this entire passage once, exactly as written:

“The next AI talk starts in twelve minutes. It is in Room C, beside the main entrance. If you want to make a call before the talk, there is a quiet area behind the café. I can take you to Room C now, or you can follow the blue signs when you are ready to go.”

Then ask:
“What did you understand? Keywords are fine.”

Collect any missing answers ONE AT A TIME:
- “When does the talk start?”
- “Where is the talk?”
- “Where can you make a call?”

Record all three answers before supplying corrections, clues, choices, or missing facts. Accept “I don’t remember.”

If I immediately request another listen, first invite me to share anything I caught. If I remember nothing, record that and continue without making me guess.

TASK 2 — Replay and check the actual gap, about 3 minutes

Ask:
“Which detail are you least sure about?”

Do not tell me its answer.

Read the SAME FULL passage again.

Ask about the exact information I was unsure of FIRST. If I forgot the call location, ask where it was, rather than asking what people can do there.

Then check any other missing information.

If I still struggle, briefly investigate ONE difficulty: unfamiliar wording, unclear sound, remembering information, or expressing an understood idea. Explain only the necessary phrase, then check understanding.

Separate answers before explanation from answers after help. If my first answers were all correct, use the replay to confirm one detail without inventing a difficulty.

If I missed a playback because I stepped away or the connection failed, repeat it and record the interruption.

TASK 3 — A short useful summary, about 1–2 minutes

Say:
“A colleague has just arrived and missed the announcement. Tell them the main arrangements in no more than two sentences.”

Let me use my own words. Give one practical improvement if needed, then one retry. Difficulty forming sentences does not automatically mean I failed to understand the announcement.

TASK 4 — Ask, choose, and explain, about 3 minutes

Continue the SAME conference situation.

First say:
“I’m the volunteer. You want help reaching the talk room. Ask me for help.”

Wait for my request without supplying a model.

Then ask:
“Would you like to make a call first, or go straight to the talk?”

Let me choose and give one reason. Either choice is acceptable.

Then ask:
“Once you reach the talk room, what do you plan to do?”

Observe whether I can express my purpose using language from Segment 1. Do not remind me of the target phrase before I answer. Accept natural alternatives.

Give focused feedback after the exchange.

TASK 5 — One brief old-expression recall, about 1 minute

Say:
“A colleague watched only the last few minutes of a recorded talk and feels confused. They have the complete video. What would you suggest so they can follow the speaker’s reasoning from the start?”

Let me answer before suggesting wording. Accept a natural recommendation. Do not require a particular word such as “entire.”

If I need help, give one small hint and, if necessary, one simple example. Record the support and move on.

ENDING — About 1 minute

Give one specific effective thing I did and one useful next focus.

Check that Tasks 1–5 actually occurred. Briefly identify unfinished actions and preserve the exact breakpoint.

Keep first-listen answers, replay answers, explanations, models and later independent responses distinct. Preserve the available conversation for the planning teacher’s review. Do not write a formal learning report.

If I pause, save the unfinished action and resume there when I return.

For now, only confirm that you are ready. Wait until I say “I’m ready for Segment 2” in Live before beginning Task 1.
