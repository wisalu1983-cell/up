# 2026-10-15 Live execution cards

Version v1. Status: not yet executed. Source: [full plan](2026-10-15.md).
Load only the active card into the immediate teaching flow.
Read [the execution protocol](../execution-protocol-live.md) before starting.
Keep a checklist; an oral completion claim is not execution evidence.

## Segment 1 — Requests and purpose

Budget: 12–15 active minutes. Speak English, naturally and slightly slowly.
Give one action at a time. Let the learner answer first.
After substantive answers, acknowledge the meaning. Offer one useful,
specific improvement when needed, then one retry. Accept natural
alternatives; keep minor grammar corrections brief.

- **S1-A, 2 min:** Ask how the learner finds their way in an unfamiliar
  place. Then ask for one real example or reason. Save natural output.
- **S1-B, 4 min:** Hotel guest wants breakfast but cannot find the
  restaurant; you are the receptionist. Ask for a short request.
  Save it, give useful feedback and any needed retry. Reply:
  “The restaurant is beside the garden. I can take you there now.”
  Let the guest respond.
- **S1-C, 3 min:** As a colleague, ask their next action and purpose for
  an AI talk in Room C. First save their own wording. If needed,
  directly teach the missing action from the full plan, confirm
  meaning, and let them express their intention once.
- **S1-D, 3 min:** New setting: conference attendee cannot find the
  registration desk; you are a volunteer. Elicit a request without a
  model. Reply: “The registration desk is straight ahead. I can show
  you.” Let the learner respond.
- **S1-E, 1 min:** Give one evidence-based strength and one next focus.
  Check every required step before reporting completion.

On pause save the exact unfinished action, next prompt, support already
given, and remaining IDs. Preserve S1-C support for the later recall.

## Segment 2 — Listen, check, and act

Budget: 12–15 active minutes. Before starting, read the fixed
[teacher material](2026-10-15-listening-teacher.md), 56 words.
Do not reveal its text or answers beforehand.

- **S2-A, 0.5 min:** Explain the volunteer scenario and three listening
  goals: start time, talk location, call location. Check readiness.
- **S2-B, 3 min:** Read the entire fixed passage. Ask what they caught;
  keywords are fine. Collect all three slots, one question at a time,
  before giving any content answer. “I don’t remember” is valid data.
- **S2-C, 3.5 min:** Ask which slot is uncertain. Replay the same whole
  passage. Check that exact gap first, then other gaps. Only afterward
  diagnose one difficulty briefly. Record all support. A missed playback
  is not a failed attentive listen.
- **S2-D, 1 min:** Ask for a summary for an arriving friend, at most two
  sentences. Separate expression difficulty from comprehension.
- **S2-E, 3 min:** Role-play a volunteer. First elicit help reaching the
  talk room. Then ask “Would you like to make a call first, or go
  straight to the talk?” Accept either choice with a reason. Finally
  ask what they plan to do once in the talk room; do not supply the
  action phrase.
- **S2-F, 2 min:** A colleague watched only the end of a recorded talk
  and is confused; the complete video is available. Elicit one useful
  suggestion without supplying a scope phrase. Then give a specific
  strength and next focus, and audit every ID.

Maintain evidence of first answers, models, retries, transfer and pending
steps. Request a Work check before a full-completion claim. If no verified
check is available, mark pending and finish the check in text. Do not
replace tasks, add unrelated expressions, or write a formal learning report.
